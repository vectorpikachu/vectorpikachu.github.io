Untyped \(\lambda\)-calculus

Exercise 1.1

Write \(M[x:=N,y:=L]\) for the simultaneous substitution: every free \(x\) and every free \(y\) in \(M\) is replaced at the same time, by \(N\) and by \(L\) respectively. On the right below stands the sequential substitution of the lecture, one variable after the other. \[ M[x:=N,y:=L] \equiv M[x:=N][y:=L] \]

Show that this is not always true, and state a condition on \(y\) and \(N\) under which the two sides do agree.
Nederpelt & Geuvers, Type Theory and Formal Proof (CUP, 2014), Exercise 1.6.

NoteDefinitions

Substitution (Def. 1.6.1). With \(\equiv\) syntactic identity, \[ \begin{aligned} x[x := N] &\equiv N, & y[x := N] &\equiv y \quad (y \neq x), \\[8pt] (PQ)[x := N] &\equiv (P[x := N])(Q[x := N]), & (\lambda y.P)[x := N] &\equiv \lambda z.P^{y \to z}[x := N]. \end{aligned} \] where in the last clause \(\lambda z.P^{y \to z}\) is an \(\alpha\)-variant of \(\lambda y.P\) with \(z \notin \operatorname{FV}(N)\), and \(P^{y \to z}\) is \(P\) with every free \(y\) renamed to \(z\).

Free variables (Def. 1.4.1). \(\operatorname{FV}(x) = \{x\}, \operatorname{FV}(PQ) = \operatorname{FV}(P) \cup \operatorname{FV}(Q), \operatorname{FV}(\lambda x.P) = \operatorname{FV}(P) \setminus \{x\}\).

Answer. Let \(M=x, N=y, L=z\). Then:

  • simultaneous substitution: \(M[x:=N,y:=L] = x[x:=y,y:=z] = y\).
  • sequential substitution: \(M[x:=N][y:=L] = x[x:=y][y:=z] = y[y:=z] = z\).

The two sides agree if \(y \notin \operatorname{FV}(N)\), i.e., if \(y\) does not occur free in \(N\).

Exercise 1.2

The first three Church numerals, and two terms mimicking addition and multiplication on them, are

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathit{zero} &:= \lambda f x.\, x, & \mathit{one} &:= \lambda f x.\, f\, x, & \mathit{two} &:= \lambda f x.\, f\,(f\,x), \\ \mathit{add} &:= \lambda m n f x.\, m\, f\, (n\, f\, x), & \mathit{mult} &:= \lambda m n f x.\, m\,(n\, f)\,x. \end{aligned} \]

  1. Show that \(\mathit{add}\ \mathit{one}\ \mathit{one} \twoheadrightarrow_\beta \mathit{two}\).
  2. Prove that \(\mathit{add}\ \mathit{one}\ \mathit{one} \neq_\beta \mathit{mult}\ \mathit{one}\ \mathit{zero}\).

Nederpelt & Geuvers, Type Theory and Formal Proof (CUP, 2014), Exercise 1.10.

NoteDefinitions

Reduction (Defs. 1.8.1, 1.8.3, 1.8.5). One step is \((\lambda x.M)N \to_\beta M[x:=N]\), applied anywhere inside a term; \(M \twoheadrightarrow_\beta N\) is a chain of zero or more such steps, and \(M =_\beta N\) is a chain in which each step may go either way.

Normal form (Def. 1.9.1). \(M\) is in \(\beta\)-normal form if it contains no redex \((\lambda x.P)Q\).

Church–Rosser (Thm. 1.9.8). If \(M \twoheadrightarrow_\beta N\) and \(M \twoheadrightarrow_\beta L\), then \(N \twoheadrightarrow_\beta R\) and \(L \twoheadrightarrow_\beta R\) for some \(R\). Hence a term has at most one \(\beta\)-normal form (Lem. 1.9.10(2)).

Answer.

  1. We show that \(\mathit{add}\ \mathit{one}\ \mathit{one} \twoheadrightarrow_\beta \mathit{two}\) by successive \(\beta\)-reductions. \[ \begin{aligned} &\mathit{add}\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{one} \\[6pt] &= (\lambda m n f x.\, m\,f\,(n\,f\,x))\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{one} \\[6pt] &\to_\beta (\lambda n f x.\, \mathit{one}\,f\,(n\,f\,x))\,\mathit{one} \\[6pt] &\to_\beta \lambda f x.\, \mathit{one}\,f\,(\mathit{one}\,f\,x) \\[6pt] &= \lambda f x.\, (\lambda g y.\, g\,y)\,f\,(\mathit{one}\,f\,x) \\[6pt] &\to_\beta \lambda f x.\, (\lambda y.\, f\,y)\,(\mathit{one}\,f\,x) \\[6pt] &\to_\beta \lambda f x.\, f\,(\mathit{one}\,f\,x) \\[6pt] &= \lambda f x.\, f\,((\lambda g y.\,g\,y)\,f\,x) \\[6pt] &\to_\beta \lambda f x.\, f\,((\lambda y.\,f\,y)\,x) \\[6pt] &\to_\beta \lambda f x.\, f\,(f\,x) \\[6pt] &= \mathit{two}. \end{aligned} \] Hence, \[ \boxed{ \mathit{add}\ \mathit{one}\ \mathit{one} \twoheadrightarrow_\beta \mathit{two}. } \]

  2. We prove that \[ \mathit{add}\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{one} \neq_\beta \mathit{mult}\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{zero}. \]

First, by part (a), \[ \mathit{add}\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{one} \twoheadrightarrow_\beta \mathit{two} = \lambda f x.\,f\,(f\,x). \]

Observe that \(\mathit{two}\) is in \(\beta\)-normal form, since it contains no \(\beta\)-redex of the form \((\lambda x.\,P)\,Q\).

Next, consider \(\mathit{mult}\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{zero}\): \[ \begin{aligned} &\mathit{mult}\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{zero} \\[6pt] &= (\lambda m n f x.\,m\,(n\,f)\,x)\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{zero} \\[6pt] &\to_\beta (\lambda n f x.\,\mathit{one}\,(n\,f)\,x)\,\mathit{zero} \\[6pt] &\to_\beta \lambda f x.\,\mathit{one}\,(\mathit{zero}\,f)\,x \\[6pt] &= \lambda f x.\,(\lambda g y.\,g\,y)\,((\lambda u v.\,v)\,f)\,x \\[6pt] &\to_\beta \lambda f x.\,(\lambda g y.\,g\,y)\,(\lambda v.\,v)\,x \\[6pt] &\to_\beta \lambda f x.\,(\lambda y.\,(\lambda v.\,v)\,y)\,x \\[6pt] &\to_\beta \lambda f x.\,(\lambda v.\,v)\,x \\[6pt] &\to_\beta \lambda f x.\,x \\[6pt] &= \mathit{zero}. \end{aligned} \]

Thus, \[ \mathit{mult}\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{zero} \twoheadrightarrow_\beta \mathit{zero}. \]

Moreover, \(\mathit{zero}=\lambda f x.\,x\) is also in \(\beta\)-normal form.

Suppose, for contradiction, that \[ \mathit{add}\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{one} =_\beta \mathit{mult}\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{zero}. \]

By the Church–Rosser theorem, \(\beta\)-equivalent terms must have the same \(\beta\)-normal form (up to \(\alpha\)-equivalence).

However, \[ \mathit{two}=\lambda f x.\,f\,(f\,x) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathit{zero}=\lambda f x.\,x \] are distinct \(\beta\)-normal forms and are not \(\alpha\)-equivalent.

This is a contradiction. Therefore, \[ \boxed{ \mathit{add}\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{one} \neq_\beta \mathit{mult}\,\mathit{one}\,\mathit{zero}. } \]
